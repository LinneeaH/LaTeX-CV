# Skills Profile

This document contains the complete set of professional skills, capabilities, domains, and natural strengths that may be referenced when adapting application material.

Items may be omitted from a CV for relevance, but should not be interpreted as less important.

---

# Interpretation Rules

This document is the canonical source for professional skills and competencies.

Unless otherwise specified, all listed items should be treated as confirmed.

## Confirmed Skills

A listed skill or capability is considered confirmed through one or more of the following:

- Professional experience
- Academic projects
- University coursework
- Independent research
- Personal projects

Professional experience is **not** required for inclusion.

## Transferable Competencies

Assume reasonable transferability between related technologies, methods, tools, and domains when supported by demonstrated experience.

Transferability should guide reasoning and recommendations, but should never be presented as direct experience.

## Clarification

If an opportunity depends on a skill, responsibility, technology, or level of experience that cannot confidently be inferred from this repository, ask targeted clarification questions before generating application material.

Examples include:

- Unlisted technologies
- Recent experience acquired after the repository was created
- Depth of experience
- Certifications
- Responsibilities that may have existed but are not documented

## Representation

When generating application material:

- Represent the complete professional profile rather than only technical competencies.
- Consider technical, analytical, collaborative, organisational, and user-centred strengths equally.
- Do not assume technical skills are more important simply because they are easier to identify.
- The absence of a skill from previous CVs does **not** imply the absence of that skill. Previous CVs are role-specific summaries, whereas this document is the canonical record of confirmed competencies.

# Natural Strengths

Recurring characteristics demonstrated across education and professional experience.

- Builds shared understanding across technical and non-technical disciplines.
- Naturally combines technical, analytical, and user-centred perspectives when solving problems.
- Quickly identifies assumptions, weaknesses, and sources of uncertainty in systems and processes.
- Enjoys understanding problems before proposing solutions.
- Structures complex information into clear and actionable forms.
- Sees connections between technical implementation, organisational context, and user needs.
- Values collaboration and performs best in cross-functional teams.
- Comfortable working across the full problem-solving process, from discovery through implementation and validation.
- Communicates technical concepts in a way that supports informed decision-making.
- Places strong emphasis on quality, robustness, reproducibility, and long-term maintainability.
- Curious and intrinsically motivated to understand underlying mechanisms rather than applying solutions mechanically.
- Adapts communication, documentation, and explanations to the audience's mental model rather than assuming shared knowledge
- Identifies implicit assumptions and makes them explicit before decisions, documentation, or implementation.
- Evaluates solutions from the perspective of the people who must use them rather than only the people who design them.
- Seeks to understand complete systems by observing multiple professions, workflows, and perspectives rather than focusing only on a single technical component.


## Learning & Adaptation

- Quickly becomes productive in new tools by mapping concepts to existing knowledge.
- Comfortable learning independently through documentation and experimentation.
- Prefers understanding underlying models and assumptions before implementation.
- Focuses on transferable concepts rather than tool-specific memorisation.
