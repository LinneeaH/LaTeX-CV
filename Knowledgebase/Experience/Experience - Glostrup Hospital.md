# Rigshospitalet Glostrup

## Overview

Rather than documenting only responsibilities, this document captures the practical experience, observations, and knowledge gained during each role. # Engineering Internship

## Role Overview

The engineering internship was completed within the Department of Clinical Physiology, Nuclear Medicine and PET at Rigshospitalet Glostrup.

The internship combined clinical engineering, medical device quality assurance, medical imaging, clinical workflow observation, and support of ongoing clinical research. Rather than focusing on a single technology or project, I worked across a broad range of imaging modalities and diagnostic equipment while collaborating with engineers, physicists, radiographers, biomedical laboratory scientists, physicians, and researchers.

The role provided practical insight into how engineering supports patient care, clinical decision-making, research, and the day-to-day operation of a modern hospital department.

---

## Original Motivation

One of my primary motivations for choosing the internship was to gain a deeper understanding of the healthcare system from the perspective of the people working within it.

Rather than only learning about medical devices academically, I wanted to understand how different professions collaborated, how clinical workflows functioned in practice, and how engineering contributes within a real healthcare environment.

I was particularly interested in seeing how technology interacted with people, workflows, and patient care rather than viewing medical devices as isolated technical systems.

---

## Responsibilities

### Medical device quality assurance

Performed scheduled quality assurance of diagnostic equipment used for both clinical examinations and research.

Quality controls were carried out according to established procedures using phantom-based and equipment-specific methods and included daily, weekly, monthly, and annual controls depending on the equipment.

Typical responsibilities included:

- Performing scheduled quality assurance procedures
- Analysing measurement results
- Comparing measurements against defined tolerances
- Identifying deviations
- Documenting results
- Escalating findings when required

---

### Medical device maintenance and repair

Participated in troubleshooting and maintenance of medical equipment throughout the department.

Whenever faults could be resolved by following service manuals or established procedures, I performed the repairs myself. More advanced faults were referred to the manufacturer, where I either coordinated the repair process or assisted visiting service engineers during on-site maintenance.

Responsibilities included:

- Fault finding
- Technical troubleshooting
- Equipment repair
- Preventive maintenance
- Supporting external service engineers
- Recognising when manufacturer support was required

---

### Clinical workflow

Observed and participated in clinical examinations throughout the department.

### Research support

Supported ongoing clinical research activities alongside the department's clinical work.

Responsibilities varied depending on the project and included technical support, image analysis, software-related tasks, and preparation of data used within clinical research.

Some of this work was carried out together with the Functional Imaging Unit (FIU), where I later continued as a Student Assistant.

## Projects / Major Contributions

### PET/CT protocol standardisation

Participated in reviewing and harmonising PET/CT examination protocols across approximately twelve scanners located at both Rigshospitalet Glostrup and Rigshospitalet Blegdamsvej.

The objective was to ensure that equivalent examinations produced comparable results regardless of scanner location while documenting differences caused by hardware generations or scanner-specific limitations.

The work included:

- Reviewing protocol parameters
- Comparing equivalent examination protocols
- Identifying inconsistencies
- Standardising protocols where appropriate
- Documenting unavoidable differences

---

### User-centred documentation

Updated and developed Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for equipment used within the department.

The work highlighted that documentation should be written for the people using it rather than for the people writing it. Procedures that appeared obvious to engineers or physicists were not necessarily intuitive for the biomedical laboratory scientists responsible for operating the equipment in daily practice.

This experience contributed to a lasting focus on:

- Adapting documentation to the intended audience
- Making implicit assumptions explicit
- Reducing ambiguity
- Supporting correct understanding rather than simply describing technical details

# Student Assistant — Functional Imaging Unit (FIU)

## Role Overview

Following the engineering internship, I continued at Rigshospitalet Glostrup as a Student Assistant in the Functional Imaging Unit (FIU), supporting ongoing clinical research projects.

Where the internship focused on understanding healthcare technology, clinical workflows, and the operation of the department, this role involved becoming an active contributor to clinical research. My responsibilities gradually expanded to cover much larger parts of the research process, from preparing participants and conducting MRI examinations to quantitative image analysis and supporting research activities.

The role provided practical experience of how clinical trials are planned, conducted, analysed, and supported by engineers working alongside clinicians and researchers.

---

## Responsibilities

### Clinical research support

Supported multiple clinical research projects, primarily involving renal MRI but also other imaging-based studies.

Responsibilities varied between projects and included:

- Preparing research examinations
- Preparing research participants
- MRI examinations
- Quantitative MRI analysis
- Scientific programming
- Technical support for researchers
- Preparing datasets for further analysis
- Supporting PhD projects and clinical studies

---

### MRI operator

Completed MRI safety training and became certified to perform MRI examinations within clinical research projects.

MRI examinations were conducted as part of a two-person team to maintain participant safety and comply with departmental procedures.

Responsibilities included:

- Receiving research participants
- MRI safety screening
- Explaining examination procedures
- Preparing participants for scanning
- Positioning participants
- Selecting and mounting appropriate coils
- Applying ECG electrodes
- Applying pulse oximetry
- Applying blood pressure monitoring equipment
- Planning imaging sequences
- Monitoring participants throughout examinations
- Managing participant wellbeing during scanning
- Transferring imaging datasets after completed examinations

As I gained experience, I was trusted with increasing responsibility and routinely participated in research scanning with relatively little day-to-day supervision while remaining within established departmental procedures.

---

### Quantitative MRI analysis

Performed quantitative analysis of renal MRI data using MATLAB, FSL, and internally developed software.

The work included analysis of multiple MRI sequences and calculation of quantitative measurements, including renal blood flow (RBF) and Q-flow.

Typical responsibilities included:

- Preparing datasets
- Processing MRI images
- Quantitative image analysis
- Comparing analytical results
- Quality checking analyses
- Supporting researchers with interpretation of technical results

---

### Understanding the complete clinical research process

One of the most valuable aspects of the role was participating in much more than the image analysis itself.

Rather than only receiving completed datasets, I became involved throughout much of the research process, from participant preparation and MRI acquisition to quantitative analysis of the resulting data.

This provided practical understanding of how earlier stages of a clinical trial influence the quality and reliability of the final analyses.

It also highlighted the close relationship between:

- Participant preparation
- Imaging protocols
- MRI acquisition
- Data quality
- Image analysis
- Research conclusions

Working with clinical trials also provided an understanding of the importance of blinding. When a treatment genuinely has an effect, knowledge of whether a participant belongs to the treatment or placebo group can unintentionally influence manual analysis and interpretation. This demonstrated why standardised procedures, objective analyses, and minimising opportunities for unconscious bias are essential in clinical research.

---

## Projects / Major Contributions

### Working with research participants

Working directly with research participants demonstrated that successful MRI examinations depend on considerably more than technically correct image acquisition.

One experience that remained particularly memorable involved guiding a blind participant through an MRI examination.

Having little previous experience assisting blind individuals, I explained this openly and asked how she preferred to be guided through the examination rather than assuming what would be most helpful.

The experience reinforced the value of asking users about their own needs rather than relying on assumptions. This approach has continued to influence how I approach stakeholder dialogue, requirements gathering, and understanding existing workflows.

## Lasting Insights

During repeated analysis of renal MRI data, I noticed that parts of the manual data analysis were surprisingly error-prone.

Even when researchers carefully followed the same written procedures, analyses—including kidney segmentations and annotations—could still vary between analysts. Existing image co-registration methods also performed differently depending on anatomy, introducing additional uncertainty into later analyses.

These observations led me to wonder whether parts of the analysis could be automated to reduce unnecessary variability and improve the reliability and reproducibility of the analyses.

That question became the starting point for my bachelor's thesis.


## Discovering Machine Learning

Before beginning the project, I had no prior experience with machine learning.

While exploring possible solutions to the challenges I had observed during the data analysis, I discovered deep learning and realised that it offered a fundamentally different approach to the problem than the traditional image processing methods I had been working with.

Wanting to understand the technology better, I chose to take my first course in **Deep Learning**.

This thesis marked the beginning of my interest in machine learning and medical image analysis.

---

## Outcome

The project resulted in a deep-learning-based method for kidney segmentation and image registration.

Following completion of the thesis, the developed method was implemented into the department's analysis workflow, where it continued to support future analyses after my employment had ended.

Seeing a solution developed during the project become part of the department's workflow demonstrated that engineering work can have practical impact beyond the duration of a project.
