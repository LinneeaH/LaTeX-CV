# BESTSELLER Logistics Centre

## Role Overview

The position was formally held through Back Up Vikar A/S as a substitute worker at BESTSELLER's logistics centre. Although employed as a substitute, the role gradually evolved into near full-time work as individual departments repeatedly requested Linnéa based on previous performance, flexibility, and reliability. Although formally employed as a substitute, I was consistently requested by specific departments and entrusted with responsibilities typically reserved for permanent staff, based on reliability, judgment, and initiative.

The logistics centre consisted of multiple departments with varying operational needs. She mainly worked in one department but has worked across every department in the logistics centre, allowing broad understanding of warehouse operations and making it possible to support whichever department required additional capacity.

A natural hierarchy existed among substitute workers. Reliable substitutes who quickly became productive and required little supervision were more likely to be requested again. Over time, Linnéa became one of the most frequently requested substitutes, both within her primary department and across other departments in the logistics centre.

# Responsibility Beyond Formal Role

One of the most formative experiences during this role occurred when a manager asked Linnéa to temporarily take responsibility for one of the smaller departments areas while a colleague was on leave.

Although this level of responsibility would normally have been assigned to a permanent employee, the manager chose Linnéa despite several permanent colleagues being available.

The decision was not requested by Linnéa but reflected the confidence the manager had developed through consistent performance, judgement, initiative, and reliability.

During these periods responsibilities included:

- Coordinating daily work
- Supporting substitute and permanent colleagues
- Monitoring workflow
- Helping ensure operational continuity
- Maintaining quality and efficiency within the department

Receiving responsibility beyond the formal expectations of the role became an important early experience that shaped confidence in handling responsibility and coordinating people without requiring formal authority.

## Lasting Insights

Several observations from this role continued to influence later education and professional work.

### Design should begin with the people doing the work

During the role, the physical layout of one department was redesigned through a major investment intended to improve operations. The redesign was planned without meaningful involvement of the employees who performed the work every day.

Although well-intentioned, the new layout made everyday work noticeably more difficult because practical workflows, constraints, and user experience had not been sufficiently understood before implementation.

This became an early lesson that has remained central throughout later education and engineering work:

Solutions should be designed only after understanding the people, workflows, and constraints they are intended to support.

This experience later aligned naturally with formal training in user-centred design, requirements engineering, medical device development, and validation during the engineering education. It also explains the continued emphasis on stakeholder dialogue, use-case discovery, observation, and understanding existing workflows before proposing technical solutions.
